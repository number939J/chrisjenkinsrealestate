The Steps

  1. You contact me. Use the form on this page or call. Tell me the address, what is wrong with the house, whether anyone lives there and your timeline.
  2. I review the property. I look at closed sales nearby and, where needed, walk the house.
  3. I give you a written offer with the price, who pays which closing costs, the closing date and the math. See how I calculate it.
  4. You decide. There is no obligation. I will also tell you what listing would likely net, so you can compare. See cash offer vs. listing.
  5. We sign a contract. The contract says in writing that I am the buyer and not acting as your agent. You are free to have an attorney or another agent review it first.
  6. Title work and closing. A title company or attorney handles closing, pays off your mortgage and any liens from the proceeds and records the deed.

What I Will Put in Writing

  • The price, the closing date and who pays which costs.
  • That I am the buyer, and not your agent, when I am buying.
  • What stays with the house and what you take.
  • Any contingencies, such as clear title.

What I Will Not Do

  • Ask for an upfront fee.
  • Pressure you to sign the same day.
  • Hide the actual buyer or sell your contract to someone else without telling you.
  • Tell you a cash sale is better when listing would net you more.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.
