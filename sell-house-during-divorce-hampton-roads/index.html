How Virginia Treats the House in a Divorce

Virginia uses equitable distribution under Va. Code § 20-107.3. A law-firm guide describes the process as classification, valuation and then distribution, and says the standard is fair, not automatically equal. The first step is classifying the house as marital property, separate property or a mix of both. A home bought before the marriage, or inherited, can be separate, while a home bought during the marriage is generally marital.

Whose name is on the deed does not settle who is entitled to what. A Virginia Court of Appeals decision reported by The Daily Record applied the equitable distribution statute rather than treating legal title as controlling.

Your Three Realistic Paths

  • One spouse keeps the house. That usually means buying out the other spouse and refinancing the mortgage into one name. The statute lets the court permit a buyout, and agreeing on it yourselves is faster.
  • List the house. The widest buyer pool and usually the highest price, but it requires showings, repairs and a buyer’s financing, and both spouses have to cooperate for months.
  • Sell directly to a cash buyer. One closing date, no showings and no repairs, usually at a lower price than a fully prepared listing.

If you cannot agree, a Virginia law firm explains that the court can transfer the property, permit a buyout or order the home sold, and that a sale is often the cleanest outcome, with the decree setting how the proceeds and costs are shared.

When a Direct Cash Sale Can Fit a Divorce

A direct sale can help when both spouses want out quickly, the house needs work neither wants to pay for, or one spouse has already moved and carrying two households is straining everyone. A cash offer is usually below what a prepared listing would bring, and that difference comes out of the money you are both dividing, so I will show you both numbers before either of you decides.

Both owners have to sign. I do not take either spouse’s side, I will not take instructions from one spouse alone, and I will put the same written offer in front of both of you.

Before You Accept Any Offer

  • Ask your attorney whether the sale should be written into your separation agreement or a court order, and how the proceeds will be divided.
  • Ask the title company or attorney handling closing how the payoff and the split will be paid, and what written instructions both of you must sign.
  • Ask a tax advisor about the tax effect of selling the home. I cannot give tax advice.
  • If the home was bought with a VA loan, read about VA loan entitlement before you sell.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.

Common Questions

Hampton Roads Cash Sale Questions, Answered

Who gets the house in a Virginia divorce?
It depends on classification and equitable distribution under Va. Code § 20-107.3. The court can transfer the property, permit a buyout or order it sold, and spouses can agree on any of these themselves.
Can one spouse sell the house without the other?
Generally both owners have to sign, and if a divorce case is pending the sale may be affected by orders or agreements. Ask your attorney before accepting any offer.
Will a cash offer hurt what I get from the divorce?
A cash offer is usually below a prepared listing, so there is less money to divide. That is a real cost, which is why I show you both numbers.
Do you work for one spouse?
No. When I am the buyer I am not acting as either spouse’s agent. Both of you should have your own attorney.
How is the money split at closing?
However your agreement or the court order says. The title company or attorney pays off the mortgage and distributes proceeds from written instructions.
What if we cannot agree on a price?
Get an independent opinion of value, or ask your attorneys about mediation. I can tell you what comparable homes sold for.
What about the mortgage if the house sells for less than we owe?
You would need your lender’s approval for a short sale, which takes longer. Start early and get legal advice.
Is this legal advice?
No. Talk with a Virginia attorney about your case.
