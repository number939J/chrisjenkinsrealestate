Can I Sell My House for Cash in Norfolk If It Needs Repairs?

Yes. A direct cash purchase does not depend on a buyer's lender, so the house does not have to meet a loan program's condition standards before closing. That is often why owners of houses with an aging roof, dated systems, water damage or an unfinished project look at a cash sale. The house still changes hands through a normal Virginia closing with a contract, title work and a settlement agent.

A cash home buyer in Norfolk, VA prices the house as it stands, including the repairs the buyer expects to take on. Chris explains how he arrives at a number on the page how a cash offer is calculated, and the we buy houses in Norfolk page covers the basics for Norfolk owners.

Do I Have to Disclose Repairs When Selling As-Is in Virginia?

Virginia's Residential Property Disclosure Act sets what owners must provide to buyers of residential property. The current form posted by DPOR, revised effective July 1, 2026, is titled a required notice for the buyer to exercise necessary due diligence. Its first item states that the owner makes no representations or warranties as to the condition of the property, and the rest of the form points the buyer to their own inspections and research on matters such as flood risk and lot lines.

Two points are worth knowing before you sell a house as-is:

  • The notice is still part of the sale. DPOR notes that certain transfers are excluded under Code of Virginia § 55.1-702, so ask the settlement agent or your attorney whether your sale is one of them.
  • Other written disclosures may apply. DPOR lists several separate disclosure sections beyond the main notice. "As-is" describes who handles repairs; it is not a way around a required disclosure.

Our guide to the Virginia residential property disclosure statement explains the form in more detail.

What If Past Repairs in Norfolk Were Done Without a Permit?

Many repair questions in Norfolk come down to permits. The City of Norfolk's Department of City Planning says a building permit is required for any residential repair, addition or new construction that requires new framing or exposes existing framing. Its list of permitted residential work includes removing drywall, moving or building walls, decks, room additions, and covering or screening porches.

The city also lists work that does not need a building permit for one- and two-family homes, including re-roofs, same-size replacement windows, vinyl or shake siding, cabinets, floor coverings and minor deck board repairs. Trade work such as plumbing, mechanical and electrical has its own applications, and a gas line needs a mechanical permit.

If you are not sure what was permitted at your house, Norfolk's planning FAQ says permit history is requested through a Freedom of Information Act request for public records. Knowing the answer helps whether you list or sell for cash, because a listing buyer's inspector may ask about it. What Closing Costs Does a Virginia Seller Pay?

One cost Virginia sellers usually see is the grantor tax. Under Code of Virginia § 58.1-802, it is 50 cents for each $500 of the consideration or value, whichever is greater, and the statute says the grantor (the seller) pays it, though the parties may arrange for the buyer to pay all or part. For example, on a $300,000 sale that works out to $300. The clerk splits the tax between the state and the locality. Other costs depend on the contract, so ask for a written estimate before you sign.

Should I Repair, List As-Is or Take a Cash Offer?

There is no single right answer. Compare these side by side:

  • Net proceeds. What you expect to receive after payoffs, costs and any repair spending, not the headline price.
  • Time. How long repairs, permits, listing prep and showings would take versus a direct sale.
  • Buyer inspections. A listing buyer may inspect and ask for repairs or credits; see our Virginia home inspection guide.
  • Certainty. How firm the closing date is and what could change it.
