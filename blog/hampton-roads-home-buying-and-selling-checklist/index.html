Hampton Roads Home Buying and Selling Checklist: Disclosures, Flood Zones, HOAs and Military Noise Zones

Hampton Roads buyer and seller checklist from REALTOR Chris Jenkins: Virginia disclosures, flood zones, HOA packets, military noise zones, and how our neighborhood stats are made.

Home Buyer Checklist for Hampton Roads

  1. Read the disclosure statement before you offer. Virginia's Residential Property Disclosure Statement is built around a notice telling buyers to do their own due diligence, so it does not replace an inspection. Our guide to the Virginia Residential Property Disclosure Statement explains each section.
  2. Use the whole inspection period. Order a general home inspection, then add specialists where the house calls for them: a wood-destroying insect report, a sewer scope, a roof or HVAC evaluation, and a radon test if the disclosure statement points you to it.
  3. Check flood zone and flood insurance by address. Flood risk varies lot by lot, even within one subdivision. Look up the specific address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, and get an insurance quote before you finish your inspection period, not after.
  4. Ask about military air installation zones. Virginia requires sellers to disclose when a property sits in a noise zone or accident potential zone around a military air installation, and the disclosure names the specific zone from the locality's official map. Hampton Roads cities with military airfields keep these maps, and it is worth looking at them yourself.
  5. Get the association documents if there is an HOA or condo association. In Virginia the association resale certificate or disclosure packet is how you learn dues, rules, reserves and any special assessments. The buyer has a limited right to cancel after receiving it, so read it as soon as it arrives.
  6. Compare against closed sales, not asking prices. Ask for recent sold homes that match the house on street, size, age and condition. Neighborhood medians are a starting point, not an appraisal.
  7. Plan the whole monthly cost. Add property tax, homeowner's and flood insurance, association dues and utilities to the mortgage payment before you decide what you can afford.

Home Seller Checklist for Hampton Roads

  1. Price from recent closed sales of similar homes. A neighborhood median hides differences in size, age, condition and lot. Chris prices each listing against the closest comparable sales.
  2. Complete your disclosure documents accurately. Virginia sets deadlines and consequences around delivering the disclosure statement, and sellers are required to disclose certain matters even in a buyer-beware state, such as a property's location in a military air installation noise or accident potential zone. Ask your agent which disclosures apply to your house.
  3. Order the association packet early. If your home is in an HOA or condo association, request the resale certificate or disclosure packet as soon as you list. Associations have a set time to deliver it after a request, and delays can slow your contract.
  4. Know your flood history and zone. Buyers and their lenders will ask. Having your flood zone, any elevation certificate and your current flood insurance premium ready answers the most common question before it becomes a negotiation.
  5. Prepare the house for photos and showings. Repairs that an inspector will find anyway are cheaper to handle before you list than after you are under contract.
  6. Choose a marketing and negotiation plan. Professional photography, accurate listing data and a clear plan for offers matter more than a high first price.

Flood Zones and Military Noise Zones in Hampton Roads

Two checks come up more in Hampton Roads than in most of the country. First, much of the region is low-lying and tidal, and Virginia leaves flood due diligence largely to the buyer. Virginia's disclosure statement points buyers to FEMA flood maps and to the Federal Emergency Management Agency's National Flood Insurance Program, and a separate Virginia disclosure covers repetitive flood loss. Second, the area has large military installations, and properties near an airfield can fall in a noise zone or an accident potential zone. Neither check is a reason to rule out a neighborhood. Both are reasons to check the specific address, because conditions differ block by block.

How the Neighborhood Statistics Are Calculated

Each neighborhood guide on this site is built from closed residential sales recorded in REIN MLS, the Hampton Roads multiple listing service, between November 15, 2024 and November 14, 2026. This is how the numbers are produced:

  • Closed sales only. Figures reflect what homes actually sold for, not asking prices or active listings.
  • Medians, not averages. A few very high or very low sales can distort an average, so price, days on market, size and year built are shown as medians. The "middle half" range runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile of sold prices.
  • Neighborhoods follow MLS subdivision names. Sales are grouped under the subdivision name agents enter in the MLS. A name can cover more than one section of a community, and some communities are listed under several names, so a guide may not match a neighborhood association's own boundaries.
  • Sample size is shown on every page. A neighborhood gets its own guide only when the MLS shows enough sales to summarize, and each page states its sales count. Where a table row shows a count under five, treat it as an observation, not a trend.
  • ZIP codes and map points. The ZIP code and map location on each guide come from running the street addresses of recent sales through the U.S. Census Bureau Geocoder and taking the most common ZIP code and the middle point of those homes. The map point marks the middle of the neighborhood's recent sales, not an official boundary. Individual addresses are used only for this step and are never published.
  • Not an appraisal. These statistics describe past sales in aggregate. They are not a valuation of any home or a prediction of future prices. For a specific house, ask Chris for a comparative market analysis.

Each guide shows the date it was last updated. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, remains the point of contact for any question about how a figure was calculated.

Common Questions

Quick Answers

What should Hampton Roads home buyers check before making an offer?
Read Virginia's Residential Property Disclosure Statement, plan a full inspection, check the specific address in FEMA's flood maps and get a flood insurance quote, look up any military noise or accident potential zone, and review HOA or condo documents if they apply. Chris Jenkins walks buyers through each step.
Does Virginia require sellers to disclose military noise zones?
Yes. Virginia law requires sellers to disclose when a property is in a noise zone or accident potential zone around a military air installation, naming the specific zone from the locality's official map. Buyers should still look at the map themselves.
What is a Virginia HOA resale certificate or disclosure packet?
It is the association's own set of documents, such as dues, rules, finances and insurance, delivered to the buyer through the seller. In Virginia the buyer has a limited right to cancel after receiving it, so buyers should read it promptly.
How are the neighborhood statistics on this site calculated?
They come from closed residential sales in REIN MLS between November 15, 2024 and November 14, 2026, grouped by MLS subdivision name and summarized with medians. Each guide states how many sales it rests on. The figures are not appraisals.
