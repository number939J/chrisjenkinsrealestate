Great Bridge, Chesapeake VA: History and Home Sales

Short answer: Great Bridge is a South Chesapeake community whose name comes from a bridge that mattered in 1775, and whose recent home sales are close to the citywide pattern. Between November 15, 2024 and November 14, 2026, 89 homes closed there with a median sold price of $429,900, according to REIN MLS records summarized on our Great Bridge neighborhood guide. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, uses both the history and the numbers when he helps people get oriented.

What Is Great Bridge, Chesapeake Known For?

Great Bridge is best known for the Battle of Great Bridge on December 9, 1775. Cardinal News describes it as the first significant land battle of the Revolution in Virginia, fought between Patriot militia and forces under Lord Dunmore, the royal governor. The Patriots won, and Dunmore soon left the area. The same article notes that the battle involved people of different races on both sides, and Chesapeake's tourism office highlights Billy Flora, a free Black man from Portsmouth, in its walking-trail story.

You can still walk that history. The Great Bridge Battlefield & Waterways Museum and Battlefield Park is at 1775 Historic Way, and Visit Chesapeake lists the battlefield trail as a stop on the city's African American Heritage Trail.

How Can You Visit the Battlefield and Museum?

If you are visiting before a showing day, here is what the museum's own site listed when this article was written on November 19, 2026. Confirm before you go, because hours and prices can change.

  • Museum hours: Tuesday through Saturday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., Sunday noon to 5 p.m., closed Mondays and some holidays.
  • Museum admission: $8 general, $7 for military and seniors, $5 for youth ages 6 to 17, free for younger children and members.
  • Park trails: open daily from sunrise to sunset at no charge.

Cardinal News also reports an annual reenactment on the first weekend of December. Check the museum's calendar for this year's schedule. Chris Jenkins often suggests that out-of-town buyers spend an hour on a walk like this, since seeing a place at street level tells you things a listing cannot.

What Do Recent Great Bridge Home Sales Show?

Closed sales show what homes actually sold for, not what sellers hoped for. Here are the main figures from the 24-month window:

  • Median sold price of $429,900, compared with $420,000 citywide, and a middle range of $345,000 to $575,000.
  • A median of 16 days on market, compared with 18 citywide.
  • About 84% detached homes and 16% townhomes or condos, with a median size of about 1,716 square feet.
  • A median year built of 1995, though sales ranged from 1957 to 2021.
  • Roughly 73% of homes closed at or above list price.

The price spread is wide. Sold prices ran from $220,000 to $1,215,000, so no single number describes the whole area. About 31% of sales fell between $300,000 and $399,999, while about 10% reached $700,000 or more. These are past results, not a forecast or an appraisal of any home.

How Does Great Bridge Compare With Nearby Chesapeake Areas?

Our guide places Great Bridge next to several South Chesapeake neighborhoods with similar median prices. Bryans Cove had a median of $424,900, Deep Creek $410,000 and Eagle Pointe $470,000 over the same period. Median days on market differed more than price did. You can read the Deep Creek guide and the Bryans Cove guide side by side, or use the neighborhood compare tool to line up price, size and home type.

What Should You Check Before Buying Near Great Bridge?

Much of Chesapeake sits near water, so flood risk is worth checking address by address. Look up the flood zone for any address and ask about an elevation certificate; our flood insurance and elevation certificate guide explains how. Our flood insurance question page covers the basics.

A short checklist for a first visit:

  1. Pull recent closed sales for the street and similar-size homes, not just the neighborhood median.
  2. Ask for the seller's disclosure, and read our disclosure statement guide first.
  3. Drive your own commute at the times you would actually travel.
  4. If you are using a VA loan, run the payment through the VA loan payment calculator.
