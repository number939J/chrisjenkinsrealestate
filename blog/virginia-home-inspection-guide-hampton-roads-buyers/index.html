Home Inspection in Virginia: Hampton Roads Buyer Guide

What a Virginia home inspection covers, what it skips, and how the inspection contingency deadline works for Hampton Roads buyers, with Chris Jenkins, REALTOR.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Published November 23, 2026

Quick answer

A Virginia home inspection is a licensed, visual review of readily accessible components, and it excludes items such as mold, radon and wood-destroying insects unless specified. Under the Virginia REALTORS inspection addendum, the buyer must act by the inspection deadline in the contract. Short answer: in Virginia, a home inspection is a buyer-ordered, visual review of the readily accessible parts of a house, and the contract deadline for it matters as much as the inspection itself. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, walks Hampton Roads buyers through the process so the inspection period does not slip by.

What Does a Home Inspection Cover in Virginia?

Home inspectors in Virginia are licensed through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR), and the regulations spell out what a written report must contain. Under 18VAC15-40-130, the report describes readily accessible components and readily observable defects in the structure (including how the crawl space was inspected), roof covering, attic and insulation, exterior, interior, plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling. It also records the date, times and weather, and it must explain any component that could not be inspected. If a system is turned off or winterized, the inspector is not required to start it, but the report has to say why it was skipped.

A few details matter to buyers: the report addresses whether readily accessible smoke alarms are in good working order, and the inspector tests the garage door auto-reverse when it can be done safely.

What Is Not Covered?

The same rules require the inspector’s contract to state that the inspection is visual, based on existing conditions, and not a code-compliance review, guarantee or warranty. The regulation lists items an inspector’s contract may exclude, such as wood-destroying organisms, mold, radon, asbestos, lead-based paint, the remaining life of components, and the cost of correcting a problem. If you want any of those checked, ask for a specialist and confirm it in writing before the inspection.

Chris Jenkins recommends reading the inspector’s agreement the day you hire them, since the exclusions list tells you what to schedule separately. In Hampton Roads, buyers often ask about crawl spaces, drainage and flood exposure; see our guide to elevation certificates and flood insurance and the question page do I need flood insurance in Hampton Roads?

How Does the Inspection Contingency Work?

Many Virginia purchase contracts use the Virginia REALTORS Home Inspection Contingency Addendum (Form 600D). According to Virginia REALTORS Legal Hotline articles, the form sets a Home Inspection Deadline by which the buyer must finish the inspection and choose whether to negotiate repairs or terminate. If the buyer chooses to negotiate, a Negotiation Period follows, and if the sides do not agree, a Purchaser’s Election Period begins. Extending the inspection deadline by amendment also extends the later periods. The exact number of days is written into your own contract, so check it rather than assuming.

The form focuses on deficiencies that could affect a reasonable buyer’s decision, not cosmetic items or personal preferences. Virginia REALTORS gives the example that a leaky roof is a deficiency, while a fresh coat of basement paint is cosmetic, and a buyer cannot end the contract simply because a seller declined a cosmetic request.

What Is the Step-by-Step Order for Buyers?

  1. Find the deadline. Mark the Home Inspection Deadline on your calendar the day the contract is ratified.
  2. Hire a licensed inspector and read their contract, including exclusions.
  3. Add specialists if the report or the property calls for them, such as a termite inspection or a flood-risk review.
  4. Sort findings into safety, structural and system issues versus cosmetic items.
  5. Decide before the deadline whether to negotiate, accept as is or terminate, and put the choice in writing.
  6. Plan the final walk-through. The Virginia REALTORS hotline notes the contract’s presettlement verification right applies within 10 days of settlement and is meant to confirm condition, not to re-inspect.

Is a Termite Inspection Required on a VA Loan?

Veterans United, a private lender, reports that a wood-destroying pest inspection is generally required on VA purchase loans in Virginia and that problems found must be cleared before closing; it also notes borrowers may pay for the report and that typical costs run roughly $100 to $200. Because that is a lender summary, confirm requirements with your own lender. Common Questions

Quick Answers

What does a home inspector check in Virginia?
A licensed inspector reports on readily accessible structure, roof, attic, exterior, interior, plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling components, per DPOR regulations. It is visual and not a code review or warranty. Chris Jenkins can help you plan add-on inspections.
Can I back out after a home inspection in Virginia?
Under the Virginia REALTORS inspection contingency, a buyer who cannot reach agreement on deficiencies can terminate within the contract's time periods, but not over cosmetic items. Check your contract's dates and ask Chris Jenkins or an attorney.
Is a termite inspection required for a VA loan in Virginia?
A private lender source reports it is generally required in Virginia and any issues must be cleared before closing. Confirm with your lender; Chris Jenkins of Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group can point you to resources.
Does a Virginia home inspection include mold or radon?
Not automatically. DPOR regulations allow inspectors to exclude mold, radon and wood-destroying organisms in their contracts, so ask for a specialist in writing.
