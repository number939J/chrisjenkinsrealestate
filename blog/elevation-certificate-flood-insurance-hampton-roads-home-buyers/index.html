Elevation Certificates and Flood Insurance in Hampton Roads

Do you need an elevation certificate to buy a home in Chesapeake or Virginia Beach? What it is, how it can affect flood insurance, and how to check an address.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Published November 17, 2026

Quick answer

An elevation certificate shows how high a home's lowest floor sits compared with expected floodwaters. Most buyers do not need one to get flood insurance, but it can be required in high-risk A or V zones and may lower a premium. Chris Jenkins of Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group helps Hampton Roads buyers check flood zones and records before making an offer.

Short answer: an elevation certificate is a document that shows how high a building sits compared with how high local floodwaters are expected to get. Most buyers do not need one just to buy flood insurance, but in Hampton Roads it is worth finding out early whether one already exists for the home you want. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, suggests asking about it before your inspection period runs out.

What Is an Elevation Certificate?

According to FEMA's FloodSmart site, the certificate records details about a building's location and characteristics, including its first floor height, which is the height of the lowest floor above the ground. A basement is not counted in that measurement, but it is noted on the form as a flood-risk indicator.

The City of Virginia Beach describes it as a tool for flood insurance rating, and as a guide for rebuilding and mitigation decisions after a disaster. It is prepared by a licensed surveyor, engineer or architect, not by the city.

Do You Need One to Buy a Home in Chesapeake or Virginia Beach?

Usually not. FloodSmart says most homeowners do not need an elevation certificate to get flood insurance. The exception is a property in a high-risk Zone A or coastal Zone V, where one may be needed to verify compliance with local safety standards. Virginia Beach says the same thing in its own words: the certificate is necessary for flood insurance in a Special Flood Hazard Area, while for moderate- to low-risk X zones it is not required for rating but may lower the rate.

Even outside those zones, FloodSmart says it is worth checking whether one is already on file. Your city or county floodplain manager can usually tell you. If none exists, you would hire a surveyor, and Virginia Beach says to expect a cost of several hundred dollars.

Can an Elevation Certificate Lower Your Flood Insurance Premium?

Maybe. FloodSmart says the certificate does not set your rate, but it can identify discounts, and generally a higher first floor means a lower chance of flood damage and a lower premium. Virginia Beach likewise says rating a policy with an elevation certificate could lower the premium. Neither page promises a specific saving, so the only way to know is to get a quote with and without the certificate from a flood insurance agent.

How Do You Check a Hampton Roads Address Before Making an Offer?

Here is a simple order of operations Chris Jenkins uses with buyers:

  1. Look up the flood zone. The FEMA Map Service Center shows flood maps. Norfolk also runs a public flood-risk site called Forerunner that shows flood zones, a property's flood risk and elevation certificates already on file.
  2. Ask the seller or agent for any existing certificate and for the Virginia residential property disclosure statement. Our disclosure statement guide explains what sellers must say about the property.
  3. Request a flood insurance quote early. Do not wait until week three of a contract.
  4. Confirm what your lender requires. FEMA says lenders generally require flood coverage for government-backed loans on buildings inside high-risk zones, which begin with A or V, and may require it in lower-risk areas too.
  5. Add the premium to your monthly budget. If you are using a VA loan, run the full number through our VA loan payment calculator.

What Do Flood Zone Letters Mean?

Norfolk's flood zone page gives a plain-language key that applies to many Hampton Roads maps. Zone X sits outside the 0.2% annual chance flood area. Shaded X is the 0.2% annual chance area. AE, AH and AO are areas with a 1% annual chance of flooding, with AE having a determined base flood elevation. VE is a coastal velocity zone with wave heights of 3 feet or more. A zone label describes the map, not a guarantee about any single storm.

Norfolk also points out that its flood risk comes from both coastal and rain flooding, and encourages coverage even where a lender does not require it. FEMA's real estate brochure adds that standard property insurance policies typically do not cover floods, and that about 40% of flood claims come from outside high-risk areas.

What About the 30-Day Waiting Period?

FEMA's brochure says a 30-day waiting period typically applies between applying for a flood policy and the effective date. There is no waiting period when the purchase is tied to making, increasing, extending or renewing a mortgage loan. It also says a seller can transfer an existing policy to the new owner so that coverage continues without a lapse. If you are not financing, or you are considering a seller's policy, ask your insurance agent to confirm how it works for your closing date.

Common Questions

Quick Answers

Do I need an elevation certificate to buy flood insurance in Virginia?
Usually not. FEMA's FloodSmart site says most homeowners do not need one, though properties in high-risk Zone A or coastal Zone V may. Chris Jenkins suggests asking your insurance agent early.
How much does an elevation certificate cost in Virginia Beach?
The City of Virginia Beach says a certificate costs several hundred dollars and is prepared by a licensed surveyor. Get quotes from surveyors for your specific property.
Can an elevation certificate lower my flood insurance premium?
It might. FloodSmart says it does not set your rate but can identify discounts, and a higher first floor generally means a lower premium. Ask an agent for quotes with and without it.
Is there a waiting period for flood insurance when buying a house?
FEMA says a 30-day waiting period typically applies, but not when the purchase is tied to a mortgage loan. Confirm timing with your insurance agent and lender, and ask Chris Jenkins to build it into your contract timeline.
Where can I check the flood zone for a Norfolk home?
Norfolk's Forerunner site shows flood zones, property flood risk and elevation certificates on file, and FEMA's Map Service Center shows flood maps.
