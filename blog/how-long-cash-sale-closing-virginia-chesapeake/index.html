What Decides How Fast a Cash Sale Closes?

Skipping lender approval removes one common source of delay, but it does not remove the legal steps of transferring a house. In practice, four things usually set the pace:

  • Title work. The settlement agent checks who owns the property and whether any liens, judgments or unpaid balances are attached to it. A clean record moves quickly; an old unreleased lien or an unclear ownership history takes time to resolve.
  • Payoffs. If a mortgage or other lien is being paid from the sale, the lender must provide a payoff figure, which is requested near closing.
  • The property. Occupancy, personal belongings, access for a walk-through and any needed repairs or documents can change the schedule.
  • Your own date. Some sellers want a closing right away; others want a later date for a move, a PCS orders date or a purchase elsewhere.

Chris Jenkins does not promise a specific number of days, because no honest buyer can know it before the title work is done. Ask for his current terms and timeline for your address.

How Does a Cash Sale Work Step by Step in Virginia?

  1. Contact and property details. You share the address, condition and your preferred timeline. See how selling to Chris Jenkins works.
  2. Written offer. You receive an offer in writing and decide with no obligation. Chris explains how he arrives at a number on the page how a cash offer is calculated.
  3. Purchase contract. The contract states the price, the closing date, who pays which closing costs and what stays with the house. You may have your own attorney or agent review it first.
  4. Settlement agent and title search. A settlement agent is chosen to handle the closing and confirm that title can transfer.
  5. Payoffs and documents. Existing liens are identified and paid from the proceeds, and the deed and settlement statement are prepared.
  6. Closing and recording. You sign, funds are disbursed and the deed is recorded.

What Does a Virginia Settlement Agent or Closing Attorney Do?

Virginia law defines escrow, closing and settlement services to include receiving and disbursing funds and handling or arranging for the recording of documents (Code of Virginia § 55.1-1000). Under § 55.1-1003, only certain people may serve as the settlement agent: a Virginia-licensed attorney, a licensed title insurance company or its appointed agent, a licensed real estate broker, or a financial institution or its affiliate, and the person must not have a disqualifying felony conviction.

The Virginia State Bar says settlement agents hold closing funds in a fiduciary capacity, deposit them in a separate escrow account by the next business day and pay them out under the parties' written instructions. It also notes that parties may choose their own settlement agent and that a non-attorney agent may not give legal advice. If you want legal advice about the sale, speak with your own attorney.

Cash Offer or Listing: What Should You Compare?

A cash, as-is sale usually trades some potential price for speed, convenience and certainty. Listing with an agent can sometimes net more money, but it generally takes more time, preparation and showings, and a financed buyer's loan may add its own conditions. Neither path is right for every house. Compare these items side by side:

  • What you expect to receive at closing after payoffs and costs, not the headline price.
  • The time from today until you have the funds, including repairs and showings if you list.
  • Whether the house needs work that a listing buyer may ask you to fix.
  • How firm the closing date is, and who pays which costs.
