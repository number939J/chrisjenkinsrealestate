Sell Your Home in Winter or Spring? Hampton Roads Guide

Should you list in winter or wait for spring? Redfin timing data, Chesapeake and Virginia Beach days on market, and seller costs, with Chris Jenkins.

Short answer: for most Hampton Roads sellers, the calendar matters less than price, condition and preparation. National data does favor the end of April for listing, but a home that is priced to recent sales can sell in any season. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, helps sellers decide whether to list this winter or prepare now and go live in spring.

When Is the Best Time to List a Home?

A Redfin analysis published April 20, 2026 looked at week-by-week patterns using 2015–2019 and 2023–2025 data (it left out 2020–2022 because of pandemic distortions). It found that homes listed at the end of April were 18% more likely to sell above original asking price than the yearly average, sold for a median price about 4% higher, and spent roughly 9% fewer days on the market. Those are national figures, and the study said timing varies by metro.

An older Redfin study of about 7 million listings from 2010 to 2014 found a smaller gap between seasons: 39% of spring listings went under contract within 30 days, and winter trailed by about one percentage point. The takeaway is that season shifts the odds a little, not completely. Neither study measures Hampton Roads on its own, so treat both as context rather than a forecast for your house.

How Fast Do Homes Sell Here Right Now?

Local closed sales are a better guide to pace. Using REIN MLS data for November 15, 2024 to November 14, 2026, the median home sold in about 18 days in Chesapeake and about 15 days in Virginia Beach, according to our pages on how long it takes to sell a house in Chesapeake and in Virginia Beach. About 44% of Chesapeake homes and 49% of Virginia Beach homes sold within 14 days, while 14% and 12% took more than 60 days.

Those medians cover every season together. They show that a well-prepared, correctly priced home can go under contract quickly, and that a minority sit for a long time. Our page on whether homes sell above asking in Hampton Roads shows how much negotiation room sellers have had.

Should You List This Winter or Wait for Spring?

There is no universal answer, but these points usually decide it:

  • Your own deadline. A PCS date, a job start or a purchase contract on another home can matter more than a few percentage points of seasonality.
  • Readiness. A home that is not repaired, cleaned and photographed is better off waiting a few weeks than going live half-finished.
  • Competition. Redfin's 2026 study found about 8% fewer homes for sale at the end of April than at the late-summer peak, so spring is not always crowded either.
  • Buyer reasons. Redfin's 2015 analysis noted that winter buyers often have pressing reasons to move, such as relocations. That can be true for military families arriving at Hampton Roads bases, but it is not a guarantee.

Chris Jenkins often suggests sellers work backward from the date they want to be under contract, then count the weeks needed for repairs, disclosures, photos and closing. If that timeline lands in late winter, the best move may be to start preparing now.

What Should You Do Before Listing?

  1. Get your pricing right. Start with recent closed sales of similar homes nearby; see how to price your home to sell in Hampton Roads.
  2. Prepare your disclosures. Virginia sellers should understand the disclosure form before listing; read our guide to the Virginia residential property disclosure statement.
  3. Gather flood and noise-zone information that buyers will ask about, using the seller checklist.
  4. Do the winter-ready basics: clear walkways, keep heat on for showings, and schedule photos in good light.
  5. Review neighborhood sales. Look at your area's guide, such as Great Bridge or Mill Creek Harbor, for sold prices and days on market.

What Does Selling Cost in Virginia?

Closing costs vary, but one item is set by statute. Under Code of Virginia § 58.1-802, the grantor (the seller) pays an additional tax of 50 cents for each $500 of the home's value, excluding any lien that stays on the property. By that formula, a $400,000 sale would carry about $400 of this tax. Buyer and seller can agree in the contract that the buyer pays some or all of it. Your closing attorney or title company will confirm the exact figures, along with commissions, payoff amounts and local fees.
