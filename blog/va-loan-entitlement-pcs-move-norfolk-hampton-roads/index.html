VA Loan Entitlement for a PCS Move to Norfolk, VA

How VA loan entitlement works for a PCS move to Norfolk and Hampton Roads: full vs. remaining entitlement, COE steps and funding fee rates, by Chris Jenkins.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Published November 16, 2026

Quick answer

VA loan entitlement is the amount the VA guarantees to your lender, and with full entitlement the VA sets no loan cap, so your price, appraisal and lender approval are the real limits. Short answer: for a PCS move to Norfolk or anywhere in Hampton Roads, your VA loan entitlement is the amount the VA is willing to guarantee for your lender. If you have full entitlement, the VA does not set a cap on how much you can borrow, so the real limits are the price, the appraised value and what a lender will approve you for. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, walks military buyers through this step before they ever tour a home.

What Is VA Loan Entitlement?

Entitlement is the VA's guarantee to your lender. It is not a limit on what you can spend. The VA's loan limits page explains that the basic amount shown on a Certificate of Eligibility is $36,000, which covers loans of $144,000 or less. For larger loans the VA guarantees up to 25% of the loan, which is called bonus entitlement. The VA says the $36,000 figure is not a borrowing limit.

Because the guarantee reduces the lender's risk, many VA loans are made with no down payment. The VA's home loan overview says nearly 90% of VA-backed loans are made without one.

Full Entitlement vs. Partial Entitlement: Which One Do You Have?

You have full entitlement if you have never used your VA loan benefit, or if you used it and the entitlement was fully restored. The VA says full entitlement does not cap loan size. The lender still has to qualify you, and the maximum VA loan is the lower of the appraised value or the purchase price.

If you still have a VA loan from a prior duty station, you may have remaining entitlement instead. For that case the VA gives this formula: remaining bonus entitlement is 25% of the county one-unit loan limit minus the entitlement you have already used. Its example uses a $900,000 county limit and $50,000 already used, which leaves $175,000 of bonus entitlement and lets a lender consider about four times that, or $700,000, with no down payment. The VA notes that most lenders want your entitlement plus any down payment to cover at least 25% of the loan.

The VA ties those county limits to the Federal Housing Finance Agency's conforming loan limits. FHFA announced on November 25, 2025 that the 2026 baseline one-unit limit is $832,750 for most of the country, with higher limits in some high-cost counties. Look up your exact county on FHFA's county list, or ask your lender, rather than assuming a number.

How Do You Get Your Certificate of Eligibility?

The VA's page on requesting a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) lists three ways, and it was last updated September 22, 2026:

  1. Online through the VA's COE request form (VA Form 26-1880).
  2. Through your lender, who may be able to pull it through the VA's Web LGY system. Ask whether yours can.
  3. By mail using VA Form 26-1880. The VA says mail requests can take longer than the other two routes.

Documents depend on your status. A Veteran needs a DD214, and an active-duty service member needs a statement of service signed by a commander, adjutant or personnel officer. With orders in hand and a short house-hunting window, requesting the COE early is one of the easiest ways to save time.

What Does the VA Funding Fee Cost?

The funding fee is a percentage of the loan amount, not the purchase price. On the VA's funding fee page (last updated October 5, 2026, with rate charts effective April 7, 2023), the purchase-loan rates for Veterans and service members are:

  • First use: 2.15% with under 5% down, 1.5% with 5% or more down, and 1.25% with 10% or more down.
  • After first use: 3.3% with under 5% down, 1.5% with 5% or more, and 1.25% with 10% or more.

The VA's own example: a first-use buyer on a $200,000 home with $10,000 down pays 1.5% of the $190,000 loan, or $2,850. Some borrowers are exempt, including those who receive VA disability compensation and active-duty members with a Purple Heart on or before closing. The page also says seller concessions are capped at 4% of the home's reasonable value and can include credits toward the funding fee. Run your own numbers in our VA loan payment calculator.

Steps for a PCS Buyer in Hampton Roads

  1. Request your COE and confirm whether you have full or remaining entitlement.
  2. Talk to a VA-experienced lender and get a pre-approval based on your full monthly cost, not just the loan amount.
  3. Compare that payment with your housing allowance. Chris Jenkins suggests including property tax, homeowner's insurance, any flood policy and HOA dues.
  4. Check each address for flood zone and military noise zone before you make an offer. Our buying and selling checklist covers both.
  5. Read about VA appraisals and inspections, and keep your own inspection. The appraisal is not one.

Then compare areas by price, commute and housing type with our neighborhood compare tool, and avoid the common mistakes military buyers make. If you want a sense of what a recent-sales snapshot looks like, see the Mill Creek Harbor guide.

Frequently Missed Points

Three things come up often. First, entitlement and the loan amount your lender approves are different questions, so a full-entitlement buyer still has to qualify on income and credit. Second, a low appraisal can change the deal, so ask your lender how it would be handled on your contract. Third, if you are keeping a prior home, tell your lender up front so they can work out your remaining entitlement. Whether and how entitlement is restored after a sale is a VA rule worth confirming directly with the VA or your lender for your situation.

Common Questions

Quick Answers

Is there a VA loan limit for a PCS move to Norfolk?
Not if you have full entitlement. The VA says full entitlement does not cap loan size, though your lender must still qualify you. With remaining entitlement, county loan limits come into play. Chris Jenkins can connect you with VA-experienced lenders.
How do I get my VA Certificate of Eligibility?
The VA lists three ways: online, through your lender, or by mailing VA Form 26-1880. Veterans need a DD214, and active-duty members need a signed statement of service.
How much is the VA funding fee in 2026?
On the VA's page, a first-use purchase loan is 2.15% with under 5% down, 1.5% with 5% or more, and 1.25% with 10% or more. After first use the under-5% rate is 3.3%. Some borrowers are exempt. Confirm current rates with your lender.
Can I use my VA loan again if I still own a home from my last duty station?
Possibly, using remaining entitlement. The VA's formula subtracts entitlement already used from 25% of the county limit. Chris Jenkins recommends telling your lender early so they can calculate it.
