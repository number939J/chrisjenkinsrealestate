VA Loan Funding Fee 2026: Hampton Roads Buyer Guide

How much the VA funding fee costs on a purchase, who is exempt, and how seller credits work for Hampton Roads buyers, with Chris Jenkins, REALTOR.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Published November 24, 2026

Home/Blog/VA Loan Funding Fee 2026: Hampton Roads Buyer Guide
Quick answer

The VA funding fee on a purchase loan is 2.15% for first use with under 5% down, 1.5% with 5% or more down and 1.25% with 10% or more, per VA.gov; veterans receiving service-connected disability compensation and certain others are exempt. Chris Jenkins of Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group helps Hampton Roads buyers estimate it before making an offer.

Short answer: the VA funding fee is a one-time charge on most VA-backed purchase loans, and for a first-time use with less than 5% down the VA lists it at 2.15% of the loan amount. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, helps Hampton Roads buyers see that number before they write an offer, because it changes the true cost of a no-down-payment purchase.

What Is the VA Funding Fee?

The funding fee is a charge paid to the Department of Veterans Affairs on a VA-backed loan. According to VA.gov (page updated October 5, 2026), you can pay it in cash at closing or finance it into the loan. On a purchase loan, it is the only fee that can be rolled into the loan amount; other closing costs are paid at closing. Lenders set their own interest rate, points and other charges, so the funding fee is only one part of your cost.

How Much Is the VA Funding Fee on a Home Purchase?

For veterans, active-duty service members, Guard and Reserve members, the VA publishes this schedule for purchase and construction loans:

  • First use, less than 5% down: 2.15%
  • First use, 5% to under 10% down: 1.5%
  • First use, 10% or more down: 1.25%
  • Later use, less than 5% down: 3.3%
  • Later use, 5% or more down: 1.5% (1.25% at 10% or more)

Veterans United and Rocket Mortgage, two private lenders, show the same purchase figures in their 2026 guides. The VA’s own example: a $200,000 home with $10,000 down (5%) and first use carries a fee of 1.5% of the $190,000 loan, or $2,850. Notice that the fee applies to the loan amount, not the price.

The “later use” row matters for military families who have used the benefit before and are now buying again after a PCS. Our guide to VA loan entitlement for a PCS move to Norfolk explains how entitlement works when you have a prior loan.

Who Is Exempt From the Funding Fee?

The VA lists several exemptions. You may not owe the fee if you:

  • receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability;
  • are eligible for that compensation but receive retirement or active-duty pay instead;
  • are a surviving spouse receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (D.I.C.);
  • are a service member with a proposed or memorandum rating before closing for a pre-discharge claim; or
  • are an active-duty member with a Purple Heart (evidence due by closing).

Veterans United notes that exemption status is confirmed on your Certificate of Eligibility or a VA benefits verification letter, and that only the VA decides exemptions. Ask your lender to check early so the right fee shows on your loan estimate.

Can You Get a Funding Fee Refund?

Possibly. The VA says a refund can apply if you are later awarded compensation with an effective date before your loan closed. A rating received only after closing does not qualify. Contact the VA regional loan center or your lender if you think this applies to you.

Can the Seller Pay the Funding Fee?

Sometimes. The VA states there is no limit on seller credits toward closing costs, but total seller concessions are capped at 4% of the home’s reasonable value shown on the VA Notice of Value, and credits toward the funding fee count against that cap. Whether a seller agrees is a negotiation, and in a Hampton Roads market where homes sell at different speeds, it varies by property. Our question page on whether homes sell above asking in Hampton Roads gives helpful context for deciding how hard to push.

How Do You Estimate the Cost Step by Step?

  1. Pick a price range and your planned down payment.
  2. Find your fee percentage from the VA table, or confirm an exemption on your Certificate of Eligibility.
  3. Multiply by the loan amount (price minus down payment).
  4. Decide whether to finance it or pay at closing.
  5. Compare monthly payments with and without the financed fee using the VA loan payment calculator.

Chris Jenkins suggests running two or three down payment scenarios, since moving from under 5% down to 5% down changes the first-use rate from 2.15% to 1.5%, according to the VA. Whether that tradeoff makes sense depends on your savings and goals, so talk with a VA lender.

The fee is not the only VA-specific step.
