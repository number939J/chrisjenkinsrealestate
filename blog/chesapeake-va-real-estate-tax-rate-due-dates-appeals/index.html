Chesapeake VA Real Estate Tax: 2026 Rate, Due Dates and Appeals

Chesapeake's real estate tax rate, the four quarterly due dates, late penalties and how to appeal a home assessment, explained by Chris Jenkins, REALTOR.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Published November 25, 2026

Quick answer

Chesapeake's real estate tax for fiscal year 2027 totals $1.01 per $100 of assessed value ($1.00 general levy plus $0.01 for mosquito control), billed in four quarterly installments, with the second quarter due December 31, 2026. Short answer: Chesapeake charges real estate tax at a combined $1.01 per $100 of assessed value for the fiscal year that began July 1, 2026, and the city bills it in four quarterly installments. The next one, the second quarter, is due Thursday, December 31, 2026, along with the second-half stormwater fee. Chris Jenkins, a REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, reviews this number with buyers before they write an offer, because it is part of the true monthly cost of owning a home in Chesapeake.

What Is the Chesapeake Real Estate Tax Rate Right Now?

The city’s tax levy ordinance for July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027 sets a general real estate levy of $1.00 per $100 of assessed value, plus a separate mosquito control levy of $0.01 per $100. The Real Estate Assessor’s Office lists the combined figure as $1.01 per hundred. The ordinance cover memo stated that it proposed no change in the real estate rate, and City Council action was scheduled for May 12, 2026. The rate can change with each annual budget, so confirm it with the city before you rely on it.

Here is a simple illustration, not a quote for any property: a home assessed at $400,000 times $1.01 per $100 equals $4,040 a year, or $1,010 per quarterly installment. Your actual bill depends on the assessed value of the specific parcel and any exemption that applies.

When Are Chesapeake Real Estate Taxes Due?

Chesapeake is different from many Hampton Roads cities because real estate tax is split into quarters. According to the Treasurer’s due-date table, the schedule is:

  • 1st quarter: September 30
  • 2nd quarter: December 31
  • 3rd quarter: March 31
  • 4th quarter: June 5

The stormwater fee is billed separately in two halves, due June 5 and December 31. When a due date lands on a weekend or holiday, the city moves it to the next business day. A city notice posted November 16, 2026 says second-quarter bills have been mailed, and that mailed payments are judged by their postmark date.

What Happens If You Pay Late?

The Treasurer’s penalty and interest page says that a tax not paid in full, or not postmarked, by the due date carries a one-time penalty of 10% of the net tax amount, plus interest at 10% per year on the tax and penalty. The city cites Virginia Code § 58.1-3915 and § 58.1-3918 for those charges. The page also states that not receiving a bill does not excuse penalty and interest, so if you have moved or just bought a home, update your mailing address with the Treasurer.

Many owners pay through a mortgage escrow account. The city’s bill-mailing page notes that state law lets a lender with a written agreement in the deed of trust receive the bill, and that if the lender does not pay, a past-due bill goes to the owner. Chris Jenkins suggests that new owners check the first bill after closing to confirm who is paying it.

How Does Chesapeake Assess Your Home?

The Assessor’s Office values all real estate every year at 100% of estimated fair market value, which it notes is required by state law. Values are set as of January 1, and new assessments take effect July 1. Owners whose value changed receive a reassessment notice, which the office says will be mailed on or before March 1, 2027. New construction, additions and demolitions are picked up through quarterly supplemental assessments.

Assessments tend to follow sales prices, so it helps to know the market. See our question pages on the median home price in Chesapeake and Chesapeake vs. Virginia Beach home prices for context.

How Do You Appeal a Chesapeake Assessment?

The Assessor’s appeal guide lays out a clear path. Here are the steps in order:

  1. Ask for an administrative review. You can request one at any time by phone, email or in person at the Assessor’s Office. Under state law, administrative appeals cover the current tax year and the three years before it.
  2. Gather evidence. The burden of proof is on the owner, who must show the assessment is above fair market value or not uniform with similar properties. Recent comparable sales and documented condition issues are the usual evidence.
  3. Apply to the Board of Equalization. Applications are accepted from March 1 through April 1 of each tax year. Mailed forms must be postmarked by April 1, hand delivery is due by 5:00 p.m. that day, and electronic or late applications are not accepted. Each parcel needs its own application.
  4. Attend the hearing. The Board meets in May, and the city says applications are heard and decided by May 31.
  5. Consider circuit court. The Assessor lists a petition to the Circuit Court as a third option, subject to time limits; ask an attorney about those deadlines.

If someone represents you, the city requires an original signed letter of authorization or a notarized power of attorney. Chris Jenkins can pull recent comparable sales to help an owner decide whether an appeal is worth pursuing, but the decision and the filing belong to the owner.

Is There a Veteran Real Estate Tax Exemption in Chesapeake?

Virginia offers a statewide real estate tax exemption for a veteran whom the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs rates as 100% service-connected, permanent and total, on the home the veteran occupies as a principal residence, covering the dwelling and up to one acre. The Virginia Department of Veterans Services says it can continue for a surviving spouse who does not remarry, and that applications go to the local Commissioner of the Revenue. Military buyers relocating to Chesapeake should ask about it early and can estimate payments with the VA loan payment calculator. How Does This Affect Buying or Selling in Chesapeake?

For buyers, the tax is a recurring cost that sits next to principal, interest and insurance. A home in Great Bridge and one in Western Branch pay the same city rate, so the difference comes from each home’s assessed value. For sellers, taxes are typically prorated at closing by the settlement agent, so ask your closing attorney how the current quarter will be split.

Quick Answers

What is the real estate tax rate in Chesapeake VA?
For the fiscal year that began July 1, 2026, Chesapeake's combined rate is $1.01 per $100 of assessed value: a $1.00 general levy plus a $0.01 mosquito control levy, per the city's tax levy ordinance and Assessor's Office.
When are Chesapeake property taxes due?
Real estate tax is due in four quarters: September 30, December 31, March 31 and June 5. If a date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.
What is the penalty for paying Chesapeake real estate taxes late?
The Treasurer charges a one-time 10% penalty on the net tax and interest at 10% per year on the tax and penalty if payment is not received or postmarked by the due date.
How do I appeal my property assessment in Chesapeake?
Start with an administrative review by the Assessor's Office, then apply to the Board of Equalization between March 1 and April 1. Chris Jenkins of Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group can provide recent comparable sales to help you decide.
Do disabled veterans pay property tax in Chesapeake VA?
Virginia exempts the principal residence of a veteran rated 100% service-connected, permanent and total by the VA, on the home and up to one acre. Apply through the Chesapeake Commissioner of the Revenue.
