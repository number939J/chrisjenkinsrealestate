Selling a Flood- or Storm-Damaged House in Hampton Roads, VA

After a flood or storm, the choice is repair, sell or both. Here is how FEMA’s damage rule works, what Virginia requires you to disclose, and how an as-is sale compares.

The 50 Percent Rule

FEMA says a residential structure is substantially damaged when repair costs equal or exceed 50 percent of its market value before the damage, in a Special Flood Hazard Area. The determination is made by a local building official or floodplain manager, not by FEMA, and land value is not counted.

A substantially damaged house generally has to be brought up to current floodplain rules when it is repaired. That can include elevating the structure, which is expensive, and it is why some owners sell instead of rebuilding. Ask your city’s floodplain manager what applies to your address.

What You Must Disclose

Selling as-is does not remove disclosure duties. See the DPOR residential property disclosure statement. Separately, an owner with actual knowledge that a dwelling is a repetitive risk loss structure must tell the purchaser. Keep photos, repair estimates, adjuster reports and claim records.

Repair, List or Sell As-Is

  • Repair, then list: may bring the most, but you need cash, contractors and time, and a substantial-damage finding can add requirements.
  • List as-is: buyers pay for the unknown, and many lenders and insurers will not finance a damaged house.
  • Direct as-is sale: no repairs and no showings, at a price that reflects the damage.

Insurance and Claims

If you have an NFIP flood policy and a substantially damaged home, FEMA notes Increased Cost of Compliance coverage may help with meeting local requirements. Talk to your adjuster before you sell, since a sale may affect what you can claim. I cannot advise on claims.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.

Common Questions

Hampton Roads Cash Sale Questions, Answered

Can I sell a flood-damaged house as-is?
Yes, with the required disclosures. The price will reflect the damage.
What is substantial damage?
In a Special Flood Hazard Area, repairs costing 50 percent or more of the pre-damage market value, as determined by local officials.
Who decides if my house is substantially damaged?
A local building official or floodplain manager, not FEMA.
Do I have to disclose past flooding?
Use the DPOR statement honestly, and disclose repetitive risk loss status if you know it.
Should I repair before selling?
Only if the repairs add more than they cost. I will tell you honestly which it is.
Will you buy a house with mold or water damage?
I look at many conditions. Tell me what you know and I will walk the property.
Does a sale affect my insurance claim?
It may. Talk to your adjuster and agent first.
Is this insurance or legal advice?
No. Talk with your insurer and a Virginia attorney.
