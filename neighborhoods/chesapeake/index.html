Home/Neighborhoods/Chesapeake, VA
At a glance

Chesapeake, VA is a Hampton Roads city made up of distinct sections such as Great Bridge, Deep Creek, Greenbrier, Western Branch, Indian River and South Norfolk. Chris Jenkins, a Chesapeake real estate agent with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, helps buyers and sellers across all of them.

How Chesapeake Is Laid Out

Chesapeake is not one single market. Home shoppers usually think in terms of its sections: Great Bridge in the central part of the city, Deep Creek and the Grassfield area to the south, Greenbrier around the Battlefield Boulevard and Greenbrier Parkway corridors, Western Branch toward Portsmouth and Suffolk, and Indian River and South Norfolk closer to Norfolk. Each has its own mix of home ages, lot sizes, association fees and commute patterns, which is why a neighborhood-level guide matters more than a citywide average.

Chris Jenkins has represented buyers and sellers in many of these areas, including Greenbrier East, Great Bridge and Indian River, according to client reviews on his reviews section.

Chesapeake Home Prices: What Sold in the Last 24 Months

REIN MLS records show 7,291 closed residential sales in Chesapeake between November 15, 2024 and November 14, 2026. The citywide median sold price was $420,000, the median home took 18 days to sell, and the median price per square foot was $219. Those citywide numbers hide big differences between neighborhoods: the median in Riverwalk was $281,750, while in Hickory it was $717,500. That is why each guide below shows the numbers for its own community.

Chris Jenkins of Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group uses closed-sale data like this, not asking prices, to price homes for sellers and to guide buyers on what a fair offer looks like.
