Sell My House As-Is in Virginia Beach: What It Really Means

Selling as-is means you will not make repairs for the buyer. It does not mean you can stay silent about problems. Here is how it works in Virginia.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Updated November 21, 2026

The short answer

In Virginia, selling as-is means no repair negotiations, but sellers must still complete the residential property disclosure or acknowledgment unless exempt, and must disclose known repetitive risk loss status. You can sell as-is by listing or to a direct cash buyer such as Chris Jenkins.

What As-Is Means and Does Not Mean

As-is means the buyer takes the house in its current condition and you agree not to repair or credit. Virginia’s residential property disclosure rules still apply. The statement, updated effective July 1, 2025, tells buyers to do their own due diligence, and sellers who have actual knowledge of a repetitive risk loss structure must disclose it.

See the plain-English disclosure guide. A Virginia attorney can advise on your situation.

As-Is Listing vs. Direct Cash Sale

  • As-is listing: widest buyer pool and the best price, but buyers will inspect, may negotiate, and financing can fall through.
  • Direct cash sale: no showings and a timeline you choose, usually at a lower price than a fully prepared listing.

Which is better depends on condition, timing and what the house would bring. Read how to price a home to sell and I will give you both numbers.

Common Virginia Beach Problems Sellers Ask About

  • Older roofs, HVAC and plumbing, common in neighborhoods built in the 1950s and 60s such as Aragona Village and Princess Anne Plaza.
  • Flood-zone and water-intrusion issues near the coast.
  • Rental properties in a Rental Inspection District.
  • Estate houses that have not been updated.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.

Common Questions

Hampton Roads Cash Sale Questions, Answered

Do I have to disclose problems if I sell as-is in Virginia?
Generally yes, unless the transfer is exempt. The seller gives the buyer the residential property disclosure statement or an acknowledgment form. As-is does not erase that.
Can a buyer back out of an as-is sale?
It depends on the contract terms, including inspection and financing contingencies. A direct cash purchase can be written with fewer contingencies, but read every term.
Will I get less selling as-is?
Usually a house sold untouched gets less than a prepared one, but you also skip repairs, carrying costs and wait time.
Do I need an attorney?
Not always, but an attorney or title company handles closing in Virginia, and an attorney can advise on legal questions.
