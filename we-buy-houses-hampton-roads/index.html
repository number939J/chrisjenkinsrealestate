Your Options When You Need to Sell Fast in Hampton Roads

Most people who search for cash home buyers in Hampton Roads end up choosing among four routes. Each one is legitimate, and each trades price for speed and convenience in a different way.

  • A local buyer who purchases directly. One person or a small company makes an offer, you agree on terms, and you close with a title company or attorney. This is the route I offer.
  • An iBuyer. A national company that makes algorithm-driven offers. These tend to work best on newer, move-in-ready homes, and their offers often include a service fee.
  • An offer marketplace or lead service. You enter your information and it is passed to investors. These services are marketing platforms, not the buyer, so ask who will actually sign the contract.
  • Listing with an agent. The widest buyer pool and usually the highest price, but the slowest route, and it means repairs, staging, showings and waiting on a buyer’s financing.

Hampton Roads has an unusual mix of sellers who need speed. The region has 18 military installations representing every branch of the U.S. military, so orders, deployments and relocations drive a steady stream of time-sensitive sales. Add inherited houses, tired rentals and homes that need more work than the owner wants to do, and a direct cash sale is the right answer for a lot of families. For others, listing is better. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can do either, and I will say which one I think fits.

How to Vet Any Cash Home Buyer, Including Me

Whoever you sell to, ask these questions before you sign. A legitimate buyer will answer all of them without pressure.

  • Who is the actual buyer? If a contract allows the buyer to assign it to someone else, find out whether you are dealing with the end buyer. Consumer guides on cash buyers recommend clarifying assignment and what happens if the final buyer cannot close.
  • Ask for proof of funds. A bank or lender letter showing the cash is available should not be a problem.
  • Get the offer in writing, including the price, who pays which closing costs, and the closing date.
  • Choose or review the closing company. In Virginia, closings are handled by a title company or attorney. You should know who it is and be free to ask questions.
  • Check the license. Anyone acting as a real estate licensee in Virginia can be looked up through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR). Check reviews for specifics, not just star ratings.
  • Never pay an upfront fee, and walk away from anyone who pushes you to sign the same day.
  • Compare at least one other path. Ask an agent what your home would likely list for, then weigh the difference against the cost and time of getting it ready.

What Changes From City to City in Hampton Roads

Hampton Roads is seven very different markets, and the details that matter in a cash sale change with the address. These are the differences I see most often.

  • Virginia Beach is the largest market and has a rental inspection program for landlords in designated districts. Owners in those districts pay a $50 fee per unit for the Certificate of Compliance inspection. See cash offers in Virginia Beach.
  • Norfolk requires a building that has been vacant for a continuous 12 months or more to be registered every year, according to the city’s registration form. See selling fast in Norfolk.
  • Chesapeake is a patchwork of sections, from older South Norfolk to new construction in Western Branch, with big price gaps between them. See we buy houses in Chesapeake.
  • Newport News and Hampton sit on the Peninsula, home to Fort Eustis and Langley Air Force Base. Hampton set up rental inspection districts by ordinance in October 2013, according to the ACLU of Virginia. See Newport News and Hampton.
  • Portsmouth is home to Norfolk Naval Shipyard and a naval medical center. See we buy houses in Portsmouth.
  • Suffolk is the largest city in Virginia by land area, at 430 square miles, with suburban neighborhoods in the north and rural land in the south. See selling fast in Suffolk.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.

Common Questions

Hampton Roads Cash Home Buyer Questions, Answered

Do you really buy houses across all of Hampton Roads?
I make cash offers in Virginia Beach, Norfolk, Chesapeake, Newport News, Hampton, Portsmouth and Suffolk. If your property is in a nearby city or county, send me the address and I will tell you honestly whether it is something I can take on.
What does it mean to sell my house as-is?
It means you do not make repairs or cleaning requests as a condition of the sale. It does not remove the disclosure rules. Virginia owners generally must give the buyer a residential property disclosure statement or sign an acknowledgment form unless the transfer is exempt, and that form tells buyers to do their own due diligence. See the DPOR disclosure statement and the plain-English guide.
How is a cash offer calculated?
I start with what similar homes have sold for nearby, then subtract the repairs and updates the house needs and the cost and risk of holding and reselling it. That is why a cash offer is usually below the price a fully prepared house could get on the open market. I will not quote a number without seeing the property, and I will show you how I got there.
How fast can you close?
As fast as clear title allows, or later if you need time. The timeline mostly depends on the title work, any loan payoff and what you need, such as extra time to move. Tell me your date and I will say honestly whether it works.
Can I sell if I still have a mortgage?
Yes. The loan is paid off from the proceeds at closing. If you owe more than the home is worth, you will need to involve your lender before any sale, and I will explain the options.
Are you an investor or a Realtor, and is there a conflict?
I am a licensed Virginia REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group. When I am the buyer, I am not acting as your agent, and I will say so in writing before you sign. You are free to talk to another agent, an attorney or a tax advisor first.
What if I have tenants in the house?
Tell me about the lease, the rent and whether the tenant is cooperative. Tenants have rights under their lease and Virginia law, so I will go over the specifics with you and, where it matters, with your attorney.
Can I sell an inherited house in Hampton Roads?
Often, yes, but only someone with legal authority can sign. In Virginia, probate is handled by the circuit court clerk in the city where the person lived. Read how it works step by step.
Can I sell my house before foreclosure?
In most cases you can sell until the foreclosure sale happens, with the sale paying off the lender. Timelines can be short, so act quickly and read this guide.
How do I know this is not a scam?
Use the checklist above: ask who the actual buyer is, ask for proof of funds, get the offer in writing, close through a title company or attorney, and verify any license on the DPOR website.
