The Problem With a Report Date

Most home sales start with a seller who can wait for the right buyer. A PCS move flips that. Your orders set a date, the new duty station sets a housing payment, and the house you are leaving keeps costing you every month it sits. The question is not simply what the house is worth. It is what each route costs you in dollars, in weeks and in risk, measured against a date you cannot move.

I am a licensed Virginia REALTOR® with Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group, and I work with Hampton Roads service members regularly. This guide lays out the real trade-offs, including the ones that favor listing, so you can decide with your own dates in front of you. For a general side-by-side, read cash offer vs. listing.

Three Routes When You Have Orders

Almost every PCS homeowner ends up choosing among selling, listing and renting, or a combination of them.

  • List it with an agent. This reaches the widest pool of buyers and usually brings the highest price. It also means preparing the house, keeping it show-ready, waiting on a buyer’s financing and inspection, and possibly handling the closing from another state. If you have several months and the house is in good shape, this is often the better financial answer.
  • Sell directly to a cash buyer. You trade some price for a closing date you choose, no showings and no repairs. This tends to fit when the report date is close, the house needs work, or you cannot be here to manage a listing.
  • Keep it and rent it. This can make sense if the numbers work and you can manage or hire out a rental from a distance. It also makes you a landlord, with a tenant, repairs and a property you cannot see. See selling a rental with tenants for what happens if you later want out.

None of these is the right answer for everyone. I will tell you which one I think fits after I see the house and your dates.

Work Backward From Your Dates

Before comparing offers, write down three dates: the day you must report, the day you need to be out of the house, and the day you would like the money in hand. Then ask each route one question: can it realistically hit those dates with a margin for something going wrong?

A listing needs time for preparation, showings, an accepted contract, any inspection or appraisal issues and closing. If that timeline is longer than the time you have, you may end up carrying two housing costs, or you may be tempted to cut the price late and under pressure. A direct sale usually has fewer moving parts, but it is still a sale: it depends on clear title, a signed contract and a title company or attorney scheduling the closing. I will say plainly what date I can and cannot support rather than promise a number before I have seen the property.

What a Cash Offer Does and Does Not Give You

A direct purchase removes the lender, the buyer’s financing contingency and, in most cases, the repair negotiation. Those are the places listings most often slip. In exchange, my offer will usually be below what a repaired, well-marketed home could fetch on the open market. You are paying for certainty and convenience, and that price is a real cost. I show you how the number is built in how I calculate a cash offer so you can judge it.

Selling as-is does not mean you skip disclosure. Read what as-is means in Virginia before you sign anything with any buyer.

A Worked Way to Compare Net Proceeds

Headline prices mislead. To compare fairly, build the same short list for each route and fill in your own numbers. Start with the expected sale price. Subtract your mortgage payoff, any liens, the closing costs the settlement agent itemizes, and any commission if you list. Then subtract what the route costs you in time: the mortgage, taxes, insurance and utilities for each month the house is not yet sold, plus any repairs or cleaning you would pay for before it goes on the market.

A listing might show a higher price but also carry months of payments and repair bills. A direct sale might show a lower price and carry almost none of those. Neither is automatically ahead until you put your numbers in. If you are weighing two housing payments, include the second payment in the listing column honestly, because that is real money leaving your account.

I am happy to build this comparison with you, using the closed sales near your address and your actual payoff. If listing would net you more, I will say so, and if you want to list, I can help you do that as your REALTOR®. That is a different relationship from a direct purchase, and I will make clear in writing which one we are in.

One more practical point: if the house is empty after you leave, read selling a vacant house for how vacancy can affect your insurance and carrying costs, and call your insurance agent before you move out rather than after.

Your VA Loan and Your Next Home

If this house was bought with a VA loan, what you do with it can affect how you buy at your next duty station. Selling, renting and keeping the loan each leave your VA benefit in a different position. I am not a lender and cannot tell you what your entitlement will look like, so read VA loan entitlement and PCS moves and confirm the details with a VA-approved lender before you choose a route. If the answer changes which option you prefer, better to learn that now than after closing.

Questions to Ask Before You Pick a Buyer

  • Who is actually signing the contract, and are you dealing with the buyer or a middleman who plans to pass the deal along?
  • What is the closing date in writing, and what has to happen for that date to hold?
  • What are you paying at closing, and what does the settlement agent show as your net proceeds?
  • Can the closing be done remotely or with a power of attorney if you have already left? Ask the title company or your attorney how they handle it. Rules and requirements are theirs to explain, not mine.
  • What stays with the house and what you must clear out?

When I am the buyer, I am not acting as your agent. I am a party to the deal with my own interest, and I will tell you that in writing. If you want representation on your side, hire your own REALTOR® or a Virginia attorney.

What to Do This Week

  1. Write down your report date, move-out date and the date you want the money.
  2. Gather your mortgage payoff estimate, your insurance information and any repair or code notices.
  3. Decide whether you could list and still hit your dates. If yes, ask for a listing estimate as a comparison.
  4. Ask for a written cash offer and compare the net numbers side by side, not the headline prices.
  5. Talk to a Virginia attorney or your base legal office about anything involving your lease, your mortgage or your legal rights as a service member.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.

Common Questions

Hampton Roads Cash Sale Questions, Answered

Should I sell or rent my house when I get PCS orders?
It depends on your numbers, your appetite for managing a rental from a distance and your plans for your VA benefit. I will walk through both with you using your actual payment, expected rent and dates.
Is a cash offer always faster than listing?
Often, but not automatically. A direct sale removes the buyer’s loan and most repair negotiation, but it still needs clear title and a closing scheduled by a title company or attorney. I will tell you honestly what date I can support.
Will I get less than if I list?
Usually, yes. A cash offer typically comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can bring. If you have time and the house is in good shape, listing often nets more. I will compare the two for you.
Can I close if I have already moved?
Ask the title company or your attorney how they handle remote closings and any power of attorney. I cannot tell you what they require, but I can help you raise the question early.
Will selling affect my next VA loan?
It can. Selling, renting and keeping the loan each affect your entitlement differently. Read our VA entitlement guide and confirm with a VA-approved lender before choosing.
I have a tenant in the house. Can I still sell?
Yes, it is possible, and the lease and deposit matter. See our guide to selling a rental with tenants and talk to a Virginia attorney about notice and lease terms.
Do I have to disclose problems if I sell as-is?
Selling as-is does not remove disclosure obligations. Review the Virginia disclosure rules and ask your attorney what applies.
Are you my agent if you buy my house?
No. When I buy, I am the buyer, not your agent. If you want representation, hire your own REALTOR® or an attorney.
What if I owe more than the house is worth?
You would need your lender’s approval for a short sale or would need to bring money to closing. Talk to your lender and an attorney before you accept any offer.
