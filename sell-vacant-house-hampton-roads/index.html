Why a Vacant House Is Expensive

You still pay taxes, utilities, insurance and upkeep on an empty house, and problems go unnoticed. Many homeowners policies include a vacancy clause that limits or suspends some coverage, typically after 30 to 60 consecutive days. GEICO notes that exact terms vary by policy, so read yours or call your agent.

Policygenius warns that a policy can be canceled if an insurer finds the home was vacant longer than allowed. A vacant home policy usually costs more.

City Rules for Vacant Buildings

Norfolk requires owners of buildings vacant for a continuous 12 months or more to register them every year, per the city’s Vacant Building Registration form. Other cities have their own code enforcement. Virginia Beach, for example, notifies owners by mail and allows from 24 hours to 30 days to correct violations depending on severity.

A vacant house with overgrown grass, broken windows or a leaking roof is the kind that draws complaints. Ask your city what applies to your address.

Your Options

  • List it. Best price if the house is in decent shape, but you pay to prepare and carry it while you wait.
  • Rent it. Turns the carrying cost into income, but you become a landlord, and some cities inspect rentals.
  • Sell as-is to a direct buyer. No repairs or showings, a closing date you choose, and the carrying costs stop at closing.

See cash offer vs. listing to compare what you would keep.

Practical Steps While You Decide

  • Call your insurance agent today about vacancy coverage.
  • Keep the lawn, mail and utilities under control so the house does not draw complaints.
  • Winterize if you are in a cold snap and the house is empty. Freezing pipes are a real risk in an empty house.
  • Gather any code notices, repair bills and the age of the roof and HVAC to give a buyer.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.
