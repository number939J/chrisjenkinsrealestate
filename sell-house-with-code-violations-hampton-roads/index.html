Selling a House With Code Violations in Hampton Roads, VA

A notice of violation does not stop you from selling, but it does not go away on its own either. Here is how Virginia code enforcement works and your options.

How a Notice of Violation Works

Under the Virginia Maintenance Code, a code official who finds violations issues a correction notice or notice of violation to the owner or the responsible person. Localities enforce it. Virginia Beach says owners are notified by mail and given from 24 hours to 30 days to correct violations, depending on severity. Norfolk lists code enforcement among its tools to achieve compliance.

What a Sale Does and Does Not Change

Do not assume a transfer ends the problem. The code says that if enforcement has begun and the owner transfers the property to an entity in which the owner holds more than a 50 percent interest, the pending action continues against the owner.

Open violations, unpermitted work and unpaid fines should be disclosed to a buyer and checked by the title company. Ask your city for the status of any case and any fines or liens before closing.

Fix, List or Sell As-Is

  • Fix, then list: works when the fixes are cheap and quick, and the city will confirm they are closed.
  • List with the violation: many buyers and lenders will not take on open violations.
  • Sell as-is to a direct buyer: I can buy a house with violations and take over the repairs, at a price that reflects the cost. Tell me what the notice says and the deadline.

Cash Sale vs. Listing With an Agent in Hampton Roads

PriceA cash offer usually comes in below what a repaired, well-marketed home can fetch on the open market. You are paying for speed, certainty and convenience.
SpeedA cash buyer does not wait on mortgage approval or a lender appraisal. A listing depends on how fast the right buyer and their financing line up.
Repairs and showingsSell as-is with no repairs, staging or open houses, versus preparing and showing the home when you list.
CertaintyFewer contingencies means fewer ways for the deal to fall through, though every sale still depends on clear title and a signed contract.
CostsNo listing commission on a direct sale, but closing costs and taxes still apply. Virginia’s state recordation tax is 25 cents per $100 of the price (Va. Code § 58.1-801), and other fees can apply. Your settlement agent itemizes all of it.

If your home is in good shape and you are not in a hurry, listing with an agent will often net you more, and I will tell you that straight. I am a full-time REALTOR®, so I can help you either way.

Common Questions

Hampton Roads Cash Sale Questions, Answered

Can I sell a house that has a notice of violation?
Yes, but the violation should be disclosed and dealt with at or before closing.
How long do I have to fix a violation?
It depends on the violation and the city. Virginia Beach says 24 hours to 30 days depending on severity.
Will the buyer inherit my fines?
Check with the city and the title company. Fines and liens should be handled at closing.
Can I avoid a violation by transferring to an LLC?
Not if you own more than 50 percent of the entity and enforcement is pending. The case continues against you.
Will you buy a house with violations?
I look at many conditions. Tell me what the notice says and what has been done.
Do I need a permit for past work?
Unpermitted work can create violations. Ask the city and a licensed contractor.
What if I disagree with the notice?
Virginia has an appeals process. Ask your city and an attorney.
Is this legal advice?
No. Talk with a Virginia attorney.
