What does a VA buyer need to know about appraisals and inspections?

VA loan appraisal vs. home inspection in Hampton Roads: what each covers, what happens if value comes in low, and questions to ask your lender.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Updated November 15, 2026

Home/Questions/What does a VA buyer need to know about appraisals and inspections?
Short answer

A VA appraisal checks that the home's value supports the price and that it meets VA minimum property requirements. It is not a home inspection, so still get your own inspection. Ask your lender how seller-paid costs and a low appraisal would work.

The VA loan is one of the best tools out there, so know how the process works before you write an offer.

Appraisal vs. Inspection

  • VA appraisal: ordered through your lender and done by a VA-assigned appraiser. It looks at whether the value supports the price and whether the home meets VA minimum property requirements.
  • Home inspection: a separate check of the home's condition, such as roof, systems, moisture and structure. The appraisal does not replace it.

If the Appraisal Comes In Low

Typical options are to renegotiate the price, make up the difference, ask for a reconsideration of value, or in some cases cancel. VA loans include protections for veterans if the value is below the price, so ask your lender how this works on your contract before you sign.

Questions for Your Lender

  1. What can the seller pay, and what limits apply?
  2. Is a termite or pest inspection required?
  3. Is my funding fee exempt or financed? Test it in the VA loan calculator.

More Quick Answers

Does a VA appraisal replace a home inspection?
No. The appraisal checks value and minimum property requirements. A home inspection looks at condition and is your own protection.
Can the seller pay my closing costs on a VA loan?
Often yes, within limits. Ask your lender for the current rules on seller-paid costs and concessions.
