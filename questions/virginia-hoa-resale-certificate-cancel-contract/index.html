What is an HOA resale certificate in Virginia, and can I cancel after reading it?

What a Virginia HOA or condo resale certificate is, what is in it, how long the association has to deliver it, and the buyer's right to cancel.

By Chris Jenkins · Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate – Native American Group · Updated November 15, 2026

Home/Questions/What is an HOA resale certificate in Virginia, and can I cancel after reading it?
Short answer

A resale certificate, also called an association disclosure packet, is the HOA or condo association's own set of documents about dues, rules and finances. Virginia gives the buyer a limited right to cancel the contract after receiving it, and an association has 14 days after a request to deliver it.

In Virginia, when you buy a home in a community governed by an association, the seller must obtain the association's resale certificate (the association disclosure packet) and give it to you. It describes the dues, rules, financial condition, insurance and other facts that affect what owning there costs.

Timing and Your Right to Cancel

  • The association has 14 days after a request from the seller or listing agent to deliver the certificate; if it does not, it is treated as unavailable.
  • The buyer has a limited right to cancel the contract after receiving the certificate, or after being notified that it will not be available. The contract states the exact window, and some standard Virginia contracts default to three days if the blank is left empty.

Read the details in your own contract, because the deadline starts on the date the certificate is delivered and a missed window can end your right to cancel.

What to Look For

Monthly or annual dues and what they cover, any special assessments, the reserve fund, pending litigation, rental and parking rules, and exterior-maintenance responsibilities. In Hampton Roads, also ask about flood insurance requirements for condos and townhomes.
